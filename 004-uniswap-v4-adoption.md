# Uniswap v4: Meaningful Adoption, or Just More Liquidity on a New Architecture?

### An evidence-based investigation into Uniswap v4 adoption, trading activity, and economic significance

**Research period:** January 1, 2026 – August 22, 2026  
**Researcher:** EMMYCRYPT  
**Category:** Protocol / Market Investigation

---

## The Question

Uniswap v4 was designed to change more than the way Uniswap pools are deployed.

Its architecture introduced a single `PoolManager`, flash accounting, and an optional hook system that allows developers to add custom logic around pool operations.

That creates a straightforward question:

> **Is Uniswap v4's new architecture translating into meaningful economic activity, or are we mainly seeing existing Uniswap trading move onto newer infrastructure?**

I wanted to answer that using observable data rather than assuming that new technology automatically means adoption.

---

## What I Looked At

This investigation focused on five areas:

- Overall v4 liquidity
- Trading volume and fees
- Distribution across chains
- Evidence of adoption
- Whether the available evidence supports claims about the economic impact of v4's architecture

I also looked specifically at **hooks**, because they are one of the features that most clearly distinguish v4 from previous Uniswap versions.

---

## What Changed With Uniswap v4?

Before looking at the numbers, it is important to understand what we are actually testing.

Uniswap v4 uses a singleton architecture in which the `PoolManager` manages pool state rather than deploying a separate contract for every pool.

The design also uses flash accounting to track balance changes during complex operations and settle the final balances at the end of a transaction.

v4 also introduced hooks.

Hooks are external smart contracts that can execute custom logic around events such as pool initialization, liquidity changes, swaps, and donations.

They can be used for things such as custom pricing, dynamic fees, custom oracles, automated liquidity management, and other specialised mechanisms.

**Sources:**

- [Uniswap v4 Architecture](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/concepts/architecture)
- [Uniswap v4 PoolManager](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v4/concepts/poolmanager)
- [Uniswap v4 Hooks](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/get-started/concepts/hooks)

# What the Data Shows

## Approximately $961.7M in TVL

As of August 22, 2026, the verified research data reports approximately:

> **$961.7 million in Uniswap v4 TVL**

That is not trivial.

It indicates that v4 has attracted substantial capital and is being used at meaningful scale.

**Source:** DeFiLlama — Uniswap v4

---

## Approximately $22.18B in 30-Day Volume

For the period from July 23 to August 22, 2026, the verified data reports approximately:

> **$22.18 billion in Uniswap v4 trading volume**

This is probably the clearest evidence that v4 is not simply an experimental deployment.

There is real economic activity taking place through the system.

**Source:** DeFiLlama — Uniswap v4

---

## Approximately $37.85M in 30-Day Fees

The same 30-day period reports approximately:

> **$37.85 million in trading fees**

This number should be treated as a measure of trading activity and fees generated by the pools.

It should **not automatically be interpreted as protocol revenue**.

That distinction matters when evaluating the economics of the protocol.

**Source:** DeFiLlama — Uniswap v4

---

# Where Is the Activity?

Uniswap v4 is deployed across multiple chains, but the available data shows that liquidity is not evenly distributed.

Ethereum accounted for approximately:

> **$713.2M of the reported $961.7M total v4 TVL**

That represents approximately:

> **74.2% of reported v4 TVL**

This means Ethereum alone represented nearly three quarters of the measured liquidity.

Other chains are participating, but simply counting the number of chains where v4 exists would give an incomplete picture of adoption.

The more useful question is where the economic activity is actually concentrated.

### Finding

> **Uniswap v4 is multichain, but its reported liquidity is heavily concentrated on Ethereum.**

---

# The Pool Concentration Question

One part of this investigation attempted to determine whether a small number of pools account for most v4 trading activity.

However, the sources reviewed did not provide sufficiently reliable data to calculate the exact percentage of volume generated by the top 5, top 10, or top 20 pools.

So I am deliberately **not publishing an estimated concentration percentage**.

Instead, the appropriate conclusion is:

> **The available evidence demonstrates concentration at the chain level, but the exact concentration of economic activity among individual pools could not be established from the verified sources used in this investigation.**

That limitation is important.

A research report should not turn an assumption into a statistic simply because the statistic would make the conclusion more interesting.

---

# What About Hooks?

Hooks are one of the most important additions introduced by v4.

They allow developers to attach custom logic to pools and can support things such as:

- Custom pricing
- Dynamic fee strategies
- Custom oracle behaviour
- Advanced order mechanics
- Automated liquidity management

However, the available verified evidence does not allow us to reliably quantify the economic importance of hooks.

We could not establish:

- The percentage of v4 pools using hooks
- The percentage of v4 trading volume involving hooks
- The amount of fees attributable to hook-based activity
- Whether hook-enabled pools systematically outperform pools without hooks

Therefore, I will not claim that hooks are driving v4 adoption.

But I also will not claim that hooks are insignificant.

The evidence simply does not establish either conclusion.

### Finding

> **Hooks are a major architectural capability of v4, but their measurable contribution to v4's economic activity remains an open research question.**

---

# Adoption vs. Architectural Impact

This is where the investigation becomes more interesting.

The data clearly supports **adoption**.

Approximately $961.7M in reported TVL and $22.18B in reported 30-day volume demonstrate substantial economic activity.

But adoption is not the same as proving that the **new architecture caused that activity**.

For example, a high-volume stablecoin pool does not automatically demonstrate that hooks, singleton architecture, or flash accounting caused that trading activity.

The trading might have occurred under an earlier Uniswap version as well.

So there are really two separate questions:

### Question 1

**Is Uniswap v4 being used?**

The evidence says:

> **Yes.**

### Question 2

**Has v4's unique architecture fundamentally changed the economics of decentralized exchange activity?**

The evidence reviewed in this investigation:

> **Does not establish that yet.**

That distinction is central to this research.

---

# What the Evidence Supports

Based on the verified information, the evidence supports the following conclusions.

### 1. Uniswap v4 has achieved meaningful usage.

The reported TVL and trading volume demonstrate substantial economic activity.

### 2. Adoption is geographically concentrated.

Ethereum represents approximately 74.2% of reported v4 TVL in the measured period.

### 3. v4 is not simply an unused experiment.

The scale of reported trading activity demonstrates that users and capital are interacting with the system at meaningful scale.

### 4. The economic impact of the architecture itself is harder to establish.

The data demonstrates **activity**, but it does not establish a causal relationship between v4's architectural features and that activity.

### 5. Hooks remain an important unanswered research question.

The architecture clearly makes hooks possible, but the verified sources reviewed do not allow their contribution to v4's economic activity to be quantified.

---

# What the Evidence Does NOT Prove

This investigation does **not** prove that:

- v4's architecture caused the reported trading volume
- hooks are responsible for a significant share of v4 activity
- a specific percentage of v4 volume comes from the top 5, 10, or 20 pools
- v4 will continue growing at the same rate
- v4 has already displaced previous Uniswap versions
- v4's economic activity necessarily translates into UNI token performance

Those claims would require additional evidence.

# Limitations

## Data Access

Much of this investigation relied on aggregated analytics rather than independently querying every underlying v4 event.

Uniswap provides v4 data infrastructure that exposes protocol-level and pool-level information, including pool count, transaction count, volume, liquidity, swaps, and historical data.

However, deeper analysis would require direct querying of those datasets.

**Source:**

[Uniswap v4 Queries](https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/ecosystem/subgraphs/concepts/v4/queries)

---

## Pool-Level Concentration

We could not obtain sufficiently reliable data to calculate the exact percentage of volume belonging to the largest pools.

Therefore, no top-5, top-10, or top-20 concentration percentage is presented as fact.

---

## Hook Activity

We could not reliably quantify:

- Hook adoption
- Hook-related trading volume
- Hook-related fees
- Whether hooks systematically improve pool performance

This means any claim about the economic impact of hooks would currently go beyond the available evidence.

---

## Causation

On-chain data can show **what happened**.

It does not automatically tell us **why it happened**.

That distinction matters.

A rise in volume occurring after the introduction of a new feature does not, by itself, prove that the feature caused the rise.

---

# Conclusion

## So, is Uniswap v4 succeeding?

The evidence says that **v4 has achieved meaningful economic activity**.

Nearly $1 billion in reported TVL and more than $22 billion in reported 30-day trading volume are difficult to describe as insignificant.

But I would stop short of saying this investigation proves that v4's new architecture has fundamentally transformed Uniswap's economics.

What the data shows more clearly is:

> **Adoption.**

What it does not yet show clearly is:

> **The incremental economic impact of the architecture itself.**

That leaves an interesting question for further research:

> **Are hooks, flash accounting, singleton architecture and other v4 features creating measurable economic advantages, or are v4's strongest markets succeeding mainly because they are already strong markets?**

Answering that properly would require deeper pool-level and transaction-level analysis.

For now, the evidence supports a more measured conclusion:

> **Uniswap v4 has meaningful usage, but the available evidence is not yet sufficient to attribute that usage directly to its distinctive architectural features.**

**Observed data**

from

**Calculated figures**

from

**Interpretation**

from
