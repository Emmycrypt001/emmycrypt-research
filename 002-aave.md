# Research #002

# AAVE: What Actually Drives the Protocol?

**Focus:** Protocol activity, economics, tokenomics, competition, value capture and risk.

Aave is one of the largest decentralized lending protocols in the research comparison set. But strong protocol activity does not automatically mean proportional value capture for the AAVE token.

This investigation examines what is actually happening underneath Aave's headline TVL.

---

## 1. Research Question

The central question is:

> **How strong is Aave's underlying protocol activity, and how directly is that activity connected to AAVE value capture?**

The research separates protocol performance from token economics rather than treating them as the same measurement.

---

## 2. Current Protocol Activity

**Research snapshot: early August 2026**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| TVL | ~$14.815B |
| Active loans | ~$11.419B |
| Calculated utilization | ~77.0% |
| 30-day gross fees | ~$29.32M |
| 30-day protocol revenue | ~$3.95M |

The calculated utilization is based on active loans divided by TVL.

Aave's activity is substantial within the comparison set.

However, these figures describe protocol activity. They do **not** establish the number of unique human users.

---

## 3. Fees vs Protocol Revenue

One of the important distinctions in this investigation is the difference between **gross fees** and **protocol revenue**.

Aave's reported 30-day figures show:

- Gross fees: ~$29.32M
- Protocol revenue: ~$3.95M
- Revenue / fees: approximately 13.5%

A simple annualized run-rate gives:

- Gross fees: ~$862.1M
- Protocol revenue: ~$112.77M

The $862.1M figure is a gross-fee run-rate, **not Aave protocol revenue**.

This distinction matters when evaluating the economic strength of the protocol.

---

## 4. Tokenomics & Value Capture

The key question is not simply whether Aave grows.

The more important question is:

> **Does protocol growth become sustainable economic value for AAVE?**

The research identifies the following pathway:

**User activity → borrowing / liquidation economics → gross fees → protocol revenue → DAO treasury → governance decision → possible AAVE demand**

The evidence supports:

- Aave generates substantial gross fees.
- A smaller amount is retained as protocol revenue.
- AAVE holders do not automatically receive a dividend-like share of protocol fees.
- The DAO has used treasury resources for AAVE buybacks.
- AAVE emissions have been reduced materially over time.

The connection between protocol activity and AAVE value capture is therefore **indirect and governance-dependent**.

---

## 5. What the Evidence Does Not Prove

The investigation deliberately avoids conclusions that the evidence cannot establish.

The available evidence does **not** prove:

- Every increase in TVL creates proportional AAVE demand.
- A historical buyback amount represents a permanent annual buyback rate.
- Buybacks caused a particular AAVE price movement.
- AAVE is permanently net-deflationary.

At approximately 220 AAVE/day, the simple annualized emission run-rate is approximately 80,300 AAVE/year.

This is an analytical run-rate, not a guarantee of future issuance.

## 6. Competitive Analysis

The research compares Aave with Morpho Blue and Compound V3.

The architectures differ, so the comparison is directional rather than perfectly like-for-like.

| Protocol | TVL | Borrowed | Chains | 30d Fees | 30d Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Aave | $14.815B | $11.419B | 23 | $29.32M | $3.95M |
| Morpho Blue | $8.05B | $4.428B | 42 | $17.54M | $0 |
| Compound V3 | $1.136B | $0.557B | 9 | $1.77M | $0.1339M |

### Read-through

Aave is substantially larger in the reported TVL comparison.

Morpho spans more chains in the research snapshot despite lower TVL.

Aave reports higher gross fees and protocol revenue in the comparison.

The important point is not simply who is larger.

The competitive question is whether Aave can maintain liquidity, borrowers, economics and product relevance as competing lending protocols evolve.

## 7. Risk Analysis

The research identifies several important risk categories:

### Smart contracts
New or modified code can contain vulnerabilities.

### Oracles
Bad pricing can produce incorrect liquidations or bad debt.

### Collateral
Collateral assets can fail, depeg or become compromised.

### Liquidity
Extreme market conditions can push utilization toward 100%.

### Bridges and external dependencies
Cross-chain failures can affect collateral assumptions.

### Governance
Parameters, treasury decisions and emissions are governance-dependent.

### Competition
Liquidity and borrowers can migrate elsewhere.

### Regulation
Changes in regulation can affect the structure of DeFi lending markets.

The research also examines an April 2026 incident involving fraudulent rsETH collateral and reports approximately $230M of Aave bad debt.

The analytical lesson is that Aave's risk surface extends beyond smart contracts to collateral, oracle, bridge and external-dependency assumptions.

Selected developments examined in the research:

This supports a **mixed** assessment rather than a simple growth or decline narrative.

## 9. Final Analytical Assessment

### Is Aave demonstrating strong underlying protocol activity?

**Yes, within the comparison set.**

Aave has approximately $14.8B TVL, $11.4B borrowed and approximately 77% calculated utilization, alongside higher reported fees and protocol revenue than the selected competitors.

### Is activity growing or declining?

**Mixed.**

The research shows modest recent TVL growth while quarterly revenue softened from the 2025 peak into 2026.

### Where is Aave strongest?

- Scale
- Lending liquidity
- Broad product coverage
- Substantial treasury and revenue base

### Where is Aave weakest?

The **token-protocol link**.

Users do not need to hold AAVE to use ordinary lending markets, and protocol growth does not automatically create proportional AAVE demand.

### Does protocol growth translate directly to AAVE value capture?

**No, not directly.**

The relationship depends on governance decisions around treasury resources, emissions and other economic mechanisms.

---

## 10. What Would Change the Thesis?

The research identifies several developments that could materially change the assessment:

- An automatic on-chain mechanism routing a defined share of fees to AAVE buybacks or burns.
- Evidence that major protocol users must hold AAVE for core functionality.
- A sustained change in treasury policy.
- A significant change in Aave's competitive position.

These are not presented as predictions.

They are variables that can be monitored against the thesis.

## 12. Research Standard

EMMYCRYPT follows a simple standard:

> **If the evidence does not prove a claim, I don't present the claim as fact.**

Where possible, the investigation distinguishes between:

- Directly observable protocol activity
- Source-reported information
- Calculated figures
- Analytical interpretation
- Uncertainty and limitations

Protocol success should not automatically be confused with token value capture.

The purpose of this research is to investigate what the evidence can — and cannot — establish.
