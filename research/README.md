Research #001

Ethereum DeFi: Is Activity Moving to L2s While Liquidity Stays on Mainnet?

Focus: Ethereum DeFi, L2 activity, liquidity, stablecoins and execution.

This investigation examines whether DeFi activity is moving toward lower-cost execution networks while capital remains concentrated on Ethereum mainnet.

The research snapshot shows Ethereum retaining substantially more reported DeFi liquidity and stablecoin supply, while some lower-cost networks record higher transaction activity.

The evidence supports a difference between where capital is concentrated and where execution occurs.

It does not, by itself, prove permanent migration of human users or economic value away from Ethereum.

Research status: Evidence-based snapshot.

Research #002

AAVE: What Actually Drives the Protocol?

Focus: Protocol activity, economics, tokenomics, competition, value capture and risk.

This research examines Aave beyond its headline TVL.

It looks at protocol activity, borrowing, utilization, fees, revenue, AAVE tokenomics, governance, competition and the relationship between protocol growth and token value capture.

The central finding is that Aave demonstrates substantial protocol activity, but the connection between that activity and AAVE value capture is indirect and governance-dependent.

Protocol growth therefore should not automatically be treated as proportional AAVE demand.

Research question:
How strong is Aave's underlying protocol activity, and how directly is that activity connected to AAVE?

Research #003

Follow the Money — An On-Chain Wallet Investigation

Focus: Solana, wallet movements, exchange flows and capital movement.

This investigation examines significant SOL movements involving staking wallets, Binance and unidentified public addresses.

The research separates directly verifiable blockchain transactions from source-reported events.

Two major Binance outflows — 374,161 SOL and 6,270,364 SOL — are directly supported by public transaction identifiers.

The investigation does not assume that an exchange withdrawal means accumulation, or that an exchange deposit means a completed sale.

The broader evidence points to a mixed flow pattern rather than a simple “whales are buying” or “whales are selling” narrative.

Key principle:
Transfer ≠ trade.

Research question:
Are significant Solana token flows showing accumulation, distribution, or a mixed pattern — and what can the available on-chain evidence actually establish?

Research status: On-chain investigation.

Research Standard

My approach is simple:

«If the evidence does not prove a claim, I don't present the claim as fact.»

Where possible, I distinguish between:

- Directly verifiable blockchain activity
- Source-reported information
- Calculated figures
- Analytical interpretation
- Uncertainty and limitations

A transaction can show that assets moved. It does not automatically explain why they moved.

Likewise, protocol activity does not automatically translate into token value capture.

The purpose of this research is to investigate the evidence and document what it can — and cannot — establish.
