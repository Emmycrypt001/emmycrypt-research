# Research #003

# FOLLOW THE MONEY

### An On-Chain Wallet Investigation — Solana Ecosystem

**Focus:** Solana wallet movements, exchange flows and capital movement.

---

## Research Question

Are significant Solana token flows showing accumulation, distribution, or a mixed pattern — and what can the available on-chain evidence actually establish?

---

## Executive Summary

This investigation looks at major SOL movements involving staking wallets, Binance and unidentified public addresses.

The strongest evidence comes from two Binance outflows:

- **374,161 SOL** on April 21, 2025
- **6,270,364 SOL** on June 28, 2026

Both transactions have public identifiers and can be independently verified.

Other events examined in this investigation include an approximately **1.373 million SOL unlock**, reported post-unlock selling, a **100,000 SOL transfer to Binance**, and reported Galaxy-linked ETH/SOL activity.

Those events are kept separate from the directly verified transactions because the available evidence does not provide enough information to independently reproduce every event.

The overall picture is not a simple "whales are buying" or "whales are selling" story.

The evidence points to a **mixed flow pattern**.

---

## What I Found

The investigation shows two different things happening at the same time.

Some reported activity points toward increased exchange-side liquidity and possible distribution.

At the same time, very large amounts of SOL moved away from Binance to unidentified public addresses.

Both observations matter.

Neither one, by itself, tells us the full economic intention behind the movement.

A wallet can receive SOL without having bought it.

A wallet can send SOL to an exchange without proving that a sale happened.

That distinction is important throughout this investigation.

### 1. 374,161 SOL — Binance → Unknown Wallet

**Date:** April 21, 2025  
**Time:** 06:15:58 UTC

**Asset:** SOL  
**Amount:** 374,161 SOL

The transaction has a public source address, destination address, timestamp and transaction signature.

What this proves:

A large amount of SOL moved from an address identified as Binance to a public destination address.

What it does not prove:

- Who controls the destination wallet
- That the recipient purchased SOL
- That the recipient is accumulating
- What the recipient intends to do with the SOL

### 2. 6,270,364 SOL — Binance → Unknown Wallet

**Date:** June 28, 2026  
**Time:** 02:57:49 UTC

The movement is significant, but the blockchain record alone does not establish whether the SOL represents:

The important point is that the movement can be verified.

The intention cannot.

## Accumulation vs Distribution

The evidence supports a **mixed flow pattern**, rather than one directional whale narrative.

### Distribution-side evidence

The April 2025 reports describe:

- A large staking unlock
- Reported subsequent selling
- A 100,000 SOL transfer to Binance

These movements are consistent with increased potential sell-side liquidity.

They do not independently prove executed market sales.

### Off-exchange movement

The two verified Binance outflows moved:

- **374,161 SOL**
- **6,270,364 SOL**

to public destinations that remain unidentified.

These withdrawals reduced the amount of SOL held in the sending exchange wallets.

That is observable.

Calling the movements "accumulation" would require additional evidence.

---

## What the Evidence Does Not Prove

The available evidence does not establish that:

- The 6.27M SOL recipient is a whale, institution or buyer.
- The 374,161 SOL recipient purchased SOL on the open market.
- The 100,000 SOL Binance deposit resulted in a sale.
- The Galaxy ETH and SOL movements were a direct Ethereum-to-Solana bridge.
- The different wallets were acting in coordination.

These are possible interpretations, but they should not be presented as established facts.

---

## Evidence Standard

EMMYCRYPT separates three things:

### 1. Directly Verifiable Evidence

Blockchain transactions with identifiable transaction information.

### 2. Source-Reported Evidence

Events reported by external sources where the underlying transaction information is not fully available for independent reproduction.

### 3. Interpretation

What the observable activity might mean.

Interpretation is not the same thing as evidence.

## The Principle

### Transfer ≠ Trade

This is one of the most important rules in this investigation.

A blockchain transaction can establish that assets moved.

It does not automatically establish why they moved.

An exchange deposit can indicate potential sell-side liquidity.

An exchange withdrawal can indicate movement away from an exchange.

Neither automatically tells us what happened next.

Intent requires additional evidence.

---

## Methodology

This investigation focuses primarily on **Solana Mainnet**, with limited contextual reference to Ethereum and Binance activity.

The evidence window covers **April 2025 through June 2026**.

Wallets were included where the available material identified exchange, staking or large-holder activity.

Unknown addresses remain unknown.

Where an address was identified as Binance by the source material, it is treated as an exchange address.

Unknown destinations are not assigned personal or institutional identities without evidence.

USD values are treated as source-reported approximations rather than exact execution prices.

---

## Limitations

The investigation does not have access to:

- Complete order-book data
- OTC trading records
- Private custody information
- Complete transaction histories for every reported event
- Evidence establishing the intentions of unidentified wallet owners

Because of these limitations, the research deliberately avoids turning wallet movements into unsupported claims about buying, selling or coordination.

---

## EMMYCRYPT Thesis

The strongest evidence does not support a simple:

**"Whales are buying."**

or

**"Whales are selling."**

narrative.

What it shows is more useful.

Significant SOL balances can move between staking addresses, exchanges and unidentified public wallets while the economic purpose of those movements remains uncertain.

Two transactions can be independently reproduced from public identifiers:

- **374,161 SOL** Binance outflow — April 21, 2025
- **6,270,364 SOL** Binance outflow — June 28, 2026

These are meaningful observations about capital movement.

They are not proof of the recipients' investment intent.

### Final conclusion

The available evidence supports a **mixed on-chain flow regime**.

Some large positions were reportedly moved toward exchange liquidity while other very large balances moved away from Binance.

The most defensible EMMYCRYPT thesis is simple:

> **Follow where the capital moved — and be honest about where the blockchain record stops being able to explain why.**
