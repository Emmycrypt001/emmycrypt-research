# Ethereum DeFi: Is Activity Moving to L2s While Liquidity Stays on Mainnet?

## Research Question

Is DeFi activity moving toward lower-cost execution networks while capital remains concentrated on Ethereum mainnet?

## Research Focus

This investigation examines the relationship between:

- DeFi activity
- Ethereum mainnet liquidity
- Layer-2 activity
- Stablecoin supply
- Transaction execution
- Capital concentration

The purpose is to determine whether increasing activity on lower-cost networks necessarily means that capital and economic activity have moved away from Ethereum mainnet.

## Evidence Examined

The research compares available indicators of:

- DeFi liquidity
- Stablecoin supply
- Network activity
- Layer-2 execution
- Ethereum mainnet activity

The investigation treats these measurements separately rather than assuming that one metric represents the entire DeFi ecosystem.

## Key Finding

The evidence indicates a difference between **where capital is concentrated** and **where blockchain execution occurs**.

Ethereum can retain substantial DeFi liquidity and stablecoin supply while lower-cost networks record significant transaction activity.

This distinction is important because high transaction activity on an L2 does not automatically demonstrate that the underlying capital base has permanently migrated away from Ethereum.

## Interpretation

The evidence supports a more careful way of looking at the Ethereum and L2 relationship.

Activity can increasingly occur on lower-cost execution networks while Ethereum remains an important location for capital and liquidity.

Therefore:

> **Execution location and capital concentration should not be treated as the same measurement.**

## What the Evidence Does Not Prove

The available evidence does not, by itself, prove:

- Permanent migration of users away from Ethereum
- Permanent migration of economic value away from Ethereum
- That higher transaction activity automatically represents greater economic value
- The future direction of Ethereum or its L2 ecosystem

These conclusions would require additional evidence and a longer observation period.

## Research Approach

The investigation follows an evidence-first approach:

1. Identify the research question.
2. Gather relevant network and market data.
3. Compare activity with liquidity and capital indicators.
4. Separate observable evidence from interpretation.
5. Document what the evidence supports and what remains uncertain.

## Conclusion

The central question is not simply whether activity is moving to L2s.

The more useful question is:

**Is execution moving while capital remains connected to Ethereum?**

The available evidence suggests that these two things can happen at the same time.

That makes the distinction between **activity, liquidity and capital concentration** important when evaluating Ethereum's evolving DeFi ecosystem.
